    07 / THE SEAT

    CFO in three years.

    Four questions a day — two technical, two not. Weighted to retail, the Gulf, and the controller-to-CFO jump. The non-technical ones decide the offer more often than the technical ones do.

    ◆ Technical
    Model the impact of a 10% AED appreciation on a UAE retailer sourcing from China.Chalhoub · Majid Al Futtaim
    Buying gets cheaper in AED terms, so landed cost falls and gross margin expands — but only after existing stock at old cost clears, so the benefit lags by roughly one inventory turn. If the AED is pegged to the USD, the real question is USD/CNY, not AED specifically. On the revenue side, a stronger currency makes the destination more expensive for tourists, which matters in Dubai where tourist spend is a meaningful share of mall retail. Net effect is usually positive on margin, mildly negative on footfall.
    A supplier offers 5% discount for payment in 10 days instead of 60. Take it?Walmart · Lulu · P&G
    Annualise it. You forgo 5% to accelerate payment by 50 days. The implied annual cost of not taking it is roughly 5/95 × 365/50 ≈ 38%. Unless your marginal cost of capital exceeds 38%, take the discount — this is one of the highest-return decisions in working capital. The caveat is liquidity: if taking it forces you onto an overdraft at 9% you still win, but if it breaches a covenant or leaves no buffer, the arithmetic is not the whole answer.
    ◆ Everything else
    How do you stay current technically while managing?Big Four partners · Group CFOs
    Be specific and verifiable. Name the standard you last read in full and why, the technical update you attended, the thing you built yourself recently rather than delegated. Vague answers about 'reading widely' land badly with technical interviewers. The strongest version connects it to a decision: 'I went back through IFRS 16 properly when we hit the lease modification question on the Indonesian stores, and that changed how we structured the renewal.'
    Where do you think retail is heading in the next five years?Gulf retail boards · Search firms
    Have a view and defend it with numbers, not trends. Something like: physical stores stop being judged on their own sales as fulfilment and returns blur the channels, which breaks conventional store P&L and forces a catchment-level view; margin pressure shifts from price to cost-to-serve; and in the Gulf specifically, corporate tax and e-invoicing professionalise finance functions that were previously light. Then say what you would do about it in the role you are interviewing for.

    Mindset · Carol Dweck · 33/48

    Fixed vs Growth Mindset: The Belief That Changes Everything

    Dweck's 30 years of research found that children (and adults) hold one of two fundamental beliefs about their abilities. Fixed mindset: abilities are innate — you're either smart or you're not. This leads to avoiding challenges (to avoid looking dumb), giving up when things get hard, and feeling threatened by others' success. Growth mindset: abilities develop through dedication and hard work. This leads to embracing challenges, persisting through setbacks, and finding inspiration in others' success. The most important finding: the mindset is not fixed. It can be changed by learning about it.

    The belief that your abilities are fixed is the only thing that makes them fixed.
    Today's actionNotice your self-talk after a failure today. Is it fixed ('I'm not good at this') or growth ('I haven't learned this yet')? The word 'yet' is the most powerful in the growth mindset.
    The whole book · 13 points
    1. Two mindsets: fixed (abilities are static) and growth (abilities develop through effort).
    2. Fixed mindset makes every task a verdict on your worth, so it avoids risk.
    3. Growth mindset treats effort and failure as the mechanism of improvement.
    4. Praising talent produces fragility. Praising process produces resilience.
    5. 'Not yet' is the most useful phrase in learning.
    6. Fixed mindset avoids challenges that might reveal limits — and so never finds them.
    7. Effort is what talent requires, not a substitute for it.
    8. Criticism is data in a growth mindset and an attack in a fixed one.
    9. Others' success is inspiration in one frame, threat in the other.
    10. Mindsets are domain-specific. You can be growth-oriented at work and fixed about maths.
    11. Fixed-mindset relationships expect compatibility; growth-mindset relationships expect work.
    12. CEO disease: fixed-mindset leaders need to be the smartest person and suppress bad news.
    13. You can change mindset by noticing the fixed voice and answering it deliberately.
    What actually changes in your head
    • How you explain a failure determines whether you get anything from it.
    • Talent praise is a trap. Process praise builds durability.
    • Avoiding challenge to protect self-image caps growth permanently.
    • 'Not yet' converts a verdict into a timeline.
    Examples
    • Dweck's students given 'you're smart' avoided harder puzzles; those praised for effort chose them and improved.
    • Michael Jordan was cut from varsity and became famous for practice volume, not gifts.
    • Lee Iacocca's fixed mindset at Chrysler: early success, then defensiveness, suppressed dissent, and decline.
    How to adapt it into your life
    • The trading ledger is a learning instrument, not a verdict. -0.029R on 1H is information, not judgement.
    • Rejection from a Dubai role is 'not yet' with a specific gap. Name the gap.
    • Praise your daughter's effort and persistence, not how clever she is. It starts this early.
    • The 8/8 APEX stop-outs were a diagnosis of the exit rule, not of your ability.

    Chess Tutor · Lesson 7/20

    Tactics: Discovered Attack

    Moving one piece reveals an attack from another. The moved piece itself may also attack something. Discovered checks are devastating — the opponent must deal with the check while you capture elsewhere. Pattern: move a blocking piece with tempo, reveal a rook/bishop attack. Double attacks are impossible to fully defend.
