    01 / INTERVIEW

    CFO in three years.

    Four questions a day — two technical, two not. Weighted to retail, the Gulf, and the controller-to-CFO jump. The non-technical ones decide the offer more often than the technical ones do.

    ◆ Technical
    Explain deferred tax to a board member in 60 seconds.Any audit committee
    Accounting profit and taxable profit are computed under different rules, so tax paid this year rarely matches tax on this year's reported profit. Deferred tax records the future consequence of that gap. The classic retail example is accelerated capital allowances on fit-out: you get the tax relief early, so you owe more tax later — that future obligation sits as a deferred tax liability. It is not cash and it is not a provision for a dispute; it is timing. Say that last sentence, because it is what they are actually worried about.
    How do you measure promotional effectiveness properly?Tesco · Carrefour · Americana
    Incremental margin, not uplift. Take the sales during the promotion, subtract what the baseline would have been without it, subtract the margin given away on units that would have sold at full price anyway, and subtract any cannibalisation of adjacent full-price lines. The result is often negative for deep discounts on already-popular SKUs. Use a control group of comparable stores where operationally possible — without a counterfactual, every promotion looks successful because sales always rise.
    ◆ Everything else
    Tell me about a time you were wrong about a person.Amazon · Unilever · Alshaya
    A quieter question than it looks — it tests self-awareness and whether you revise judgements. Both directions work: someone you underestimated who turned out strong, or someone you backed who did not work out. The second is braver and lands better if you own the hiring or promotion decision. End with what you changed about how you assess people, ideally something structural like a different interview question or a probation checkpoint.
    If we hire you and in a year it has not worked, what is the most likely reason?Senior search processes
    A genuinely good question and a rare one. Answer honestly with the real risk, not a disguised strength: a mismatch on decision-making pace, an unclear boundary between your scope and someone else's, or a business where the data foundations are worse than described so the first year goes on plumbing rather than insight. Then say what you would do in month one to check for it. Naming the failure mode makes you look like someone who has thought about the job rather than the offer.

    Never Split the Difference · Chris Voss · 37/48

    Tactical Empathy: The Most Powerful Negotiation Tool

    FBI hostage negotiator Chris Voss's core technique: tactical empathy. Not sympathy (feeling what they feel) but empathy (understanding what they feel and why). In any negotiation, the other party has emotional needs underneath their stated position. Label those emotions: 'It seems like you're frustrated by the timeline.' 'It sounds like you feel the value isn't there.' Labelling emotions defuses them and builds trust. When people feel understood, they are more open to creative solutions. The biggest mistake in negotiation is treating it as purely rational — it is almost always emotional first.

    The fastest path to yes in any negotiation is to make the other person feel genuinely understood first.
    Today's actionIn your next difficult conversation, try one label: 'It seems like...' or 'It sounds like...' and see what happens to the tone.
    The whole book · 14 points
    1. Negotiation is not rational argument. It is emotional intelligence applied under pressure.
    2. Tactical empathy: recognise and name the other side's perspective without agreeing to it.
    3. Mirroring — repeat their last 1–3 words — keeps them talking and reveals information.
    4. Labelling: 'It seems like…', 'It sounds like…'. Naming a negative defuses it.
    5. Run an accusation audit — list their worst assumptions about you and say them first.
    6. 'No' is the start of the negotiation, not the end. It creates safety.
    7. Aim for 'That's right', not 'You're right'. The second means they want you to stop.
    8. Never split the difference. Compromise produces outcomes neither side wanted.
    9. Deadlines are usually artificial and almost always work against whoever fears them.
    10. The F-word: fairness. When they invoke it, they feel wronged. Address that, not the maths.
    11. Bend their reality with anchoring, loss aversion, and non-round numbers.
    12. Calibrated 'How' and 'What' questions transfer the problem without confrontation.
    13. 'How am I supposed to do that?' is the most useful sentence in the book.
    14. Find the Black Swan — the unknown that reframes the whole deal. Three types of leverage: positive, negative, normative.
    What actually changes in your head
    • Listening is the tactic, not the courtesy.
    • Getting to 'no' early saves months of false progress.
    • Non-round numbers read as calculated; round numbers read as arbitrary.
    • Compromise is usually mutual loss wearing a diplomatic label.
    Examples
    • Voss talked down a Brooklyn bank robber using mirroring alone, with no tactical leverage available.
    • The Chase Manhattan hostage case: 'It seems like you don't want to go back to jail' opened a conversation that demands had closed.
    • A student negotiated a salary from $30k to $34k plus a defined promotion path by asking 'How am I supposed to accept that?'
    How to adapt it into your life
    • Dubai salary talk: anchor with a non-round number — AED 32,400, not 30,000. Then ask how they can get there.
    • Accusation audit before the offer conversation: 'You're probably thinking I'm asking above the band for this level.'
    • Label the objection about not having UAE experience before they raise it.
    • Never split the difference on the SIP amount. Automate the number and remove the negotiation with yourself.

    Chess Tutor · Lesson 11/20

    Strategy: Open Files for Rooks

    Rooks need open files to be active. An open file has no pawns. A semi-open file has only enemy pawns. Double rooks on an open file (called 'Rooks on 7th') is a deadly battery. Strategy: open files by pawn trades, then occupy them immediately. Don't leave your rooks passive on the back rank.
