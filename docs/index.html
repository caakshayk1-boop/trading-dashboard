05 / THE SEAT

CFO in three years.

Four questions a day — two technical, two not. Weighted to retail, the Gulf, and the controller-to-CFO jump. The non-technical ones decide the offer more often than the technical ones do.

◆ Technical
How would you decide between renting and buying a distribution centre?Amazon · Lulu · DP World
Compare NPV of both over the same horizon at the same discount rate. Rent is an operating commitment — now on balance sheet under IFRS 16 anyway — with flexibility to exit. Buying ties up capital and carries residual value risk, but fixes occupancy cost and creates an asset. The real question is strategic, not arithmetic: how confident are you in the network design over 15 years. If the answer is 'not very', pay the premium for optionality.
UAE corporate tax is 9%. How does that change your group structure thinking?PwC Gulf · Chalhoub · Emaar
It ends the era of treating the UAE as a zero-tax base. Key points: 9% applies above AED 375K taxable income; qualifying free zone persons can still get 0% on qualifying income but the conditions are strict and substance-tested; transfer pricing documentation is now mandatory and OECD-aligned. Practically: intercompany charges between a UAE HQ and operating markets need defensible benchmarking, and management fee structures designed purely for tax will be challenged. Model the effective rate, not the headline rate.
◆ Everything else
Why should we hire you over a candidate with Big Four and listed-company experience?Every shortlist
Do not disparage the alternative. Frame the trade: audit teaches you to verify what happened; operating finance teaches you to change what happens next. Then be specific about what you own that they do not — a live multi-country P&L, an ERP implementation you led rather than advised on, and store-level decisions you made and were held to. End with the honest gap and how you would close it. Confidence with a named weakness beats confidence alone.
How do you influence people who do not report to you?Landmark Group · Alshaya · Carrefour
Retail finance is almost entirely this — Buying, Operations and Retail Heads do not report to you. The answer is currency: work out what each function is measured on and frame your ask in their terms. A buyer cares about sell-through and margin, not about your working capital target — so bring them the SKU-level analysis showing which lines tie up cash without turning. Give people the analysis they cannot build themselves, and influence follows. Name a specific instance and the outcome.

Start With Why · Simon Sinek · 29/48

The Golden Circle: Why, How, What

Most companies communicate from the outside in: what they do, then how they do it, then why. Apple communicates from the inside out: why first (we believe in challenging the status quo), then how (by making beautifully designed, simple products), then what (we make computers and phones). The why speaks to the limbic brain — the emotional, decision-making centre. The what speaks to the neocortex — logical but not the driver of decisions. People don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it. This is why Apple users are more loyal than Dell users, even when specs are comparable.

People don't buy what you do. They buy why you do it. And they don't care about your what until they believe your why.
Today's actionWrite your personal WHY in one sentence: 'I exist to _____ so that _____.' Does your daily work reflect this?
The whole book · 13 points
  1. The Golden Circle: Why → How → What. Most communicate outside-in; leaders work inside-out.
  2. People don't buy what you do. They buy why you do it.
  3. Why is a belief or purpose. How are the differentiating actions. What are the products.
  4. The Golden Circle maps to biology: What is neocortex (rational), Why is limbic (feeling, trust, loyalty).
  5. That is why 'it feels right' outperforms a superior spec sheet.
  6. Manipulations — price, promotions, fear, novelty — produce transactions, not loyalty.
  7. Clarity, discipline, consistency: know why, hold yourself to how, and be consistent in what.
  8. The Law of Diffusion of Innovation: you need 15–18% adoption before the majority moves.
  9. Sell to the people who believe what you believe first. They are the 2.5% who tolerate imperfection.
  10. Why comes from your past, not from a strategy session. It is discovered.
  11. The Celery Test: given your Why, filtering decisions becomes obvious to observers.
  12. Split of Why happens when the What outgrows the belief. Founders' clarity fades and the company drifts.
  13. Why attracts the right people. Skills can be taught; belief cannot.
What actually changes in your head
  • Purpose is a filter, not a slogan. It should visibly kill options.
  • Trust is limbic. It does not respond to feature lists.
  • Early believers are worth more than early volume.
  • If your Why isn't reflected in what you refuse, you don't have one.
Examples
  • Apple sells the same components as Dell but leads with challenging the status quo — and can sell phones and music while Dell could not sell MP3 players.
  • The Wright brothers beat Samuel Langley, who had funding, Smithsonian backing, and press. They had belief and a team that shared it.
  • King's 'I have a dream' — not 'I have a plan'. 250,000 arrived without invitations.
How to adapt it into your life
  • Your Why: most financial advice ignores behaviour, so it fails. That belief explains the content, the products, and the refusals.
  • The Celery Test kills motivational finance content instantly. It doesn't fit the Why.
  • Sell Codex to the 2.5% who already believe automation beats hiring. Not to the majority.
  • Lead the Dubai pitch with the belief, not the CV bullet list. The bullets are the What.

Opening Principle #3: Castle Early

King safety is non-negotiable in the opening. Castle within the first 10 moves. An uncastled king in the centre is a target. After castling, your king is protected by 3 pawns and 2 corner squares. Rooks also connect after castling. Rule: castle before you attack.
