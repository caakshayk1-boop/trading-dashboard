Essentialism · Greg McKeown · 27/48

The 90% Rule: If It's Not a Hell Yes, It's a No

McKeown's 90% rule for decisions: when evaluating an opportunity, give it a score from 0 to 100 on fit with your most important criteria. If it scores below 90, say no. This sounds extreme. In practice, it forces clarity: most things we say yes to are 60-70% fits — good enough, not great. The 60% fits crowd out the 90% opportunities that actually advance your most important goals. Every yes is an implicit no to everything else. The question is not 'is this a good opportunity?' but 'is this the best opportunity I have right now?'

Every yes costs you a no somewhere else. Make sure the trade is worth it.
Today's actionApply the 90% rule to your next opportunity or request. Score it honestly. If it's below 90, practice saying a polite no.
The whole book · 13 points
  1. Almost everything is noise. Very few things are exceptionally valuable.
  2. The undisciplined pursuit of more is how capable people stall.
  3. Replace 'How can I do it all?' with 'What is the one thing I should do?'
  4. If you don't prioritise your life, someone else will.
  5. Trade-offs are unavoidable. Pretending otherwise means choosing badly by default.
  6. Explore widely before committing narrowly. More options examined, fewer selected.
  7. Apply extreme criteria: not 'is this good?' but 'is this the best possible use of this time?'
  8. 'No' is a complete sentence. The discomfort of refusing lasts minutes; the cost of agreeing lasts months.
  9. Sunk cost is not a reason. Ask what you would pay to acquire this commitment today.
  10. Buffer time by 50%. Every plan without slack fails on contact with reality.
  11. Remove the bottleneck rather than working harder around it.
  12. Small wins beat big launches. Progress is the most effective motivator known.
  13. Essentialism is a discipline, not a one-time purge. The noise regrows weekly.
What actually changes in your head
  • Saying yes to everything is a decision — usually a bad one made passively.
  • The cost of a commitment is every alternative you can no longer take.
  • Capable people get buried precisely because they are capable.
  • Slack is not waste. Slack is what absorbs reality.
Examples
  • Southwest Airlines said no to meals, assigned seating, and hub routing — and stayed profitable for decades while full-service rivals collapsed.
  • Warren Buffett attributes most of his wealth to roughly ten decisions, having passed on thousands.
  • McKeown attended a client meeting hours after his daughter's birth. He got neither the client's respect nor the moment back — the book's founding regret.

Opening Principle #1: Control the Centre

Place pawns on e4/d4 (White) or e5/d5 (Black). Pieces control more squares from the centre. A pawn on e4 controls d5 and f5. A pawn on the edge controls only 1 square. Open with 1.e4 or 1.d4 and you immediately claim space. This is the foundation of all chess strategy.
