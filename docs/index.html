    How this screen works — and what it is not
    A good company is not the same as a good buy today. That is what Rank for is: the four component scores below never change, but each mode weights them for a different question. Investor leans on business quality and price and almost ignores the chart. Positional wants compounding that is also working now. Swing is the chart with the fundamentals only as a floor. The same name can be a 53 to an investor and an 83 to a swing trader — that gap is the useful part, and averaging it into one number is what this section is trying to stop doing.

    Four scores, deliberately not one. A company can be excellent and expensive; a chart can be strong while the business degrades. Collapsing that into a single number destroys the only thing you need in order to disagree with it. So Quality is return on capital, margins and leverage; Growth is three-year compounding of revenue and EBITDA; Value is how the stock is priced against its own industry peers, not against the market; Tech is the chart alone, with no fundamental input. The composite is a declared weighted blend of those four (35/25/25/15), renormalised over whichever of them exist for that company.

    On ROCE. It is computed here from the published income statement and balance sheet — operating profit over capital employed — not read off a field, because the data source does not publish one. For lenders it is left blank rather than approximated: a bank’s capital employed is its deposit base, so the ratio means nothing there and a number would be worse than a dash. Where the accounts do not support a figure, the cell is empty and the score it feeds is computed from fewer inputs, marked with a dotted underline.

    What this is not. A ranking of public data, not advice, and not a prediction — there is no probability, target or forecast anywhere in it, because nothing here has been validated as predictive. I am not a SEBI-registered adviser.

    1. 2026-08-09 SELECTION adopted

      Stop buying strength that has already run

      Evidence

      The 4H RSI ceiling for longs was 75. Splitting every closed trade by the RSI recorded at entry showed the engine bleeding above 65 and profitable below it. The ceiling is now 65.

      BUY, 4H RSI 65 and over −0.599R n=90 −5.6 SE

      Holds in both halves of the sample and at every cut point tested (60, 65, 70), so it is not one lucky threshold. The threshold was still chosen from this sample, so treat the size as an upper bound.

    2. 2026-08-08 STOPS rejected

      A break-even stop would not have helped

      Evidence

      The median losing trade was +1.44R in profit before it reversed, and 59% touched a full +1R first. That looks like an obvious fix: move the stop to entry once price pays 1R. Re-walking every trade bar by bar, with winners subject to the same rule, says otherwise.

      baseline +0.194R
      break-even @1R +0.166R worse

      The first version of this test only altered losers, which guarantees a positive answer. Correcting it reversed the conclusion. Nothing tested cleared one standard error, so no stop rule was adopted.

    3. 2026-08-08 INTEGRITY adopted

      Trades were being closed at prices that never traded

      Evidence

      The grader had never resolved a single trade — an exception was being swallowed silently — leaving a fallback path that could book an exit at a bar from before the signal existed. Every closed trade was re-graded against bars that actually printed.

      This moved the headline number against me. It is published anyway, because the earlier number was not real. Pre-correction data is kept in the repo rather than deleted.
