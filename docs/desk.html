    02 / CFO TRACK

    CFO in three years.

    Four questions a day — two technical, two not — plus two field notes. Weighted to retail, the Gulf, and the controller-to-CFO jump. The non-technical ones decide the offer more often than the technical ones do; the field notes are the things nobody asks you and everybody assumes you already know.

    ◆ Technical
    Gross margin fell 180bps. Walk me through the bridge.Target · Tesco · Max Fashion
    Decompose into four drivers: mix — did the sales split shift toward lower-margin categories; price — did you discount more, measured as markdown as a percent of gross sales; cost — did landed cost rise from FX, freight or supplier price; and shrink/wastage. Quantify each in basis points so they sum to 180. The bridge is the answer, not the total. In retail the most common culprit is markdown depth on aged stock, which is really an buying error surfacing one season late.
    Auditors propose an adjustment you disagree with. What do you do?Any listed group
    Separate judgement from fact. If it is a factual error, show the evidence and it goes away. If it is a judgement — provisioning, impairment, lease term — set out your basis in a technical memo: the standard, the specific paragraph, the assumptions and their support. Escalate to the engagement partner if needed, and involve the audit committee chair early rather than late. If they still disagree and it is material, you record it. What you never do is trade an adjustment against a different one.
    ◆ Everything else
    Tell me about a time you were wrong about a person.Amazon · Unilever · Alshaya
    A quieter question than it looks — it tests self-awareness and whether you revise judgements. Both directions work: someone you underestimated who turned out strong, or someone you backed who did not work out. The second is braver and lands better if you own the hiring or promotion decision. End with what you changed about how you assess people, ideally something structural like a different interview question or a probation checkpoint.
    If we hire you and in a year it has not worked, what is the most likely reason?Senior search processes
    A genuinely good question and a rare one. Answer honestly with the real risk, not a disguised strength: a mismatch on decision-making pace, an unclear boundary between your scope and someone else's, or a business where the data foundations are worse than described so the first year goes on plumbing rather than insight. Then say what you would do in month one to check for it. Naming the failure mode makes you look like someone who has thought about the job rather than the offer.
    ◆ From the field
    The controller who never leaves the close never becomes CFO.The single most common plateau
    The job you are promoted out of is 'the person who produces the numbers'. The job you are promoted into is 'the person who decides what to do about them'. If your calendar is 70% close, reconciliation and audit prep, you are demonstrating that you are excellent at the job you already have. Push the close down: a well-run retail group closes in 5 working days, and every day you shave buys back roughly 12 days a year of capacity to spend on decisions instead of production.
    The Gulf pays for scarcity, not for years.Compensation structure
    Salary in the region tracks the intersection of skills, not the length of the CV. CA plus genuine FP&A plus retail plus a system implementation is a much rarer combination than any one of them is deep. If you are negotiating, the leverage is in naming the intersection, not in listing the years. And check the structure: basic versus allowances changes gratuity, which is calculated on basic salary alone.

    Wealth Is What You Don't See

    We judge wealth by what we see: cars, clothes, houses. But wealth is actually the money not spent — it's invisible. The person driving a ₹1 crore car might have zero savings. The person in the modest apartment might have ₹10 crore liquid. Housel's key insight: spending money to show people how much money you have is the fastest way to have less money. True wealth is options — the ability to wake up and say 'I can do what I want, when I want, with who I want.' That requires unspent money, not displayed money.

    Rich is a current income. Wealthy is accumulated assets. They are not the same.
    Today's actionTrack your net worth monthly, not your lifestyle. Net worth = financial freedom. Lifestyle = just looking free.
    The whole book · 14 points
    1. Doing well with money has little to do with intelligence and everything to do with behaviour.
    2. No one is crazy. Every money decision makes sense given that person's specific history of experience.
    3. Luck and risk are the shadow siblings of every outcome. Neither is measurable, both are decisive.
    4. There is no reason to risk what you have for what you don't need.
    5. Compounding is counterintuitive: $81.5bn of Buffett's $84.5bn arrived after his 65th birthday.
    6. Getting money and keeping money are different skills. The second requires frugality and paranoia.
    7. Tails drive everything. A minority of events account for the majority of outcomes.
    8. The highest dividend money pays is control over your own time.
    9. Wealth is what you don't see. Spending is evidence of spending, not of wealth.
    10. Save without a specific goal. Savings buy optionality against a world you cannot forecast.
    11. Room for error is the single most underrated force. Plan for the plan not going to plan.
    12. Avoid extreme financial commitments — your future self's goals will not match your current ones.
    13. Volatility is the fee for high returns, not a fine for a mistake. Fees you pay willingly; fines you avoid.
    14. Define the game you are playing, and stop taking cues from people playing a different one.
    What actually changes in your head
    • A mediocre strategy held for 30 years beats a brilliant one abandoned in year 3.
    • Most financial mistakes are emotional, so most financial fixes must be behavioural.
    • Survival is the prerequisite for compounding. Everything else is secondary.
    • Your risk tolerance is not a number. It is what you actually do at a 40% drawdown.

    • Ronald Read, a petrol-station attendant, left $8m by buying blue chips for decades. Richard Fuscone, Harvard MBA and Merrill executive, went bankrupt in 2008.

    Endgame: King Activity

    In the endgame, the king becomes a fighting piece. Centralise your king immediately. A king on e4 controls d3, d4, d5, e3, e5, f3, f4, f5 — 8 squares. A king on a1 controls only 3. Rule: when queens are off the board, march your king to the centre. Opposition matters: two kings facing each other, one move apart — the side NOT to move has the opposition.
