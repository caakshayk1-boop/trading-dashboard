The ONE Thing · Gary Keller · 28/48

What's the ONE Thing That Makes Everything Else Easier?

Keller's focusing question: 'What is the ONE Thing I can do, such that by doing it, everything else becomes easier or unnecessary?' This question forces prioritisation at the deepest level. Most to-do lists are lists of tasks with no hierarchy. The focusing question demands you find the task that is the linchpin — the one that, if done, makes other tasks unnecessary or simpler. Applied to a business: the one customer that unlocks others. Applied to a career: the one skill that opens all doors. Applied to a day: the one call that makes the rest of the day productive.

Extraordinary results are directly determined by how narrow you can make your focus.
Today's actionEvery morning, before opening email, answer: 'What is the ONE Thing I can do today that would make everything else easier?' Do that first.
The whole book · 12 points
  1. The focusing question: what's the ONE thing I can do, such that by doing it everything else gets easier or unnecessary?
  2. Success is sequential, not simultaneous. One domino at a time.
  3. A 2-inch domino can topple one 50% larger. Twenty-three dominoes in sequence reach the moon.
  4. Multitasking is a lie. The brain task-switches, and each switch costs time and accuracy.
  5. Willpower is a battery, not a trait. Spend it on the ONE thing, early.
  6. A balanced life is a myth. Counterbalance instead: go extreme on priorities, then return.
  7. Big is not bad. Thinking small caps the outcome before you start.
  8. Time-block the ONE thing first — four hours, daily, protected before anything else enters the calendar.
  9. Protect the block like a surgeon protects theatre time. Interruption is not negotiable.
  10. Goal-set to the now: someday → 5-year → 1-year → month → week → today.
  11. Productivity is not doing more things. It is doing the right thing until it compounds.
  12. Four thieves: inability to say no, fear of chaos, poor health habits, an environment that doesn't support the goal.
What actually changes in your head
  • Priorities plural is a contradiction. The word had no plural for 500 years.
  • Extraordinary results come from narrow, sustained focus — not from breadth.
  • If everything is important, nothing gets finished.
  • Chaos elsewhere is the price of excellence somewhere. Accept it consciously.
Examples
  • Gary Keller rebuilt Keller Williams by replacing 14 executives and focusing on one thing at a time; it became the largest real-estate firm in the US.
  • Pat Matthews sold one painting after a year, then committed to one painting per day. Within two years he was selling at $10,000 each.
  • The Colonel spent years on one recipe and one method before franchising anything.
How to adapt it into your life
  • Weekly: name the ONE thing that makes the Dubai offer likelier. Usually it is one specific application done properly, not ten sent.
  • Time-block 6–8am for it. Everything else takes what remains.
  • On TradeFlow: ship the measured equity engine well before adding a fifth page.
  • Counterbalance, don't balance. Job-hunt sprints will cost evenings. Say so out loud and repay the time after.

Rule of 72

Years to double = 72 ÷ rate. At 12%, six years. At 8%, nine. Lets you sanity-check any growth or return claim instantly, without a spreadsheet.

The repApply it to five return rates. Then invert: what rate doubles in 4 years?

Chess Tutor · Lesson 2/20

Opening Principle #2: Develop Your Pieces

In the first 10 moves, move each piece once. Get knights out before bishops (Ng1-f3, Nb1-c3). Don't move the same piece twice. Don't bring the queen out early — she gets chased. Goal: by move 8, have 3-4 pieces off the back rank. Development = time. Time = initiative.
