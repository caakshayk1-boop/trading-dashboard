    02 / CFO TRACK

    CFO in three years.

    Four questions a day — two technical, two not — plus two field notes. Weighted to retail, the Gulf, and the controller-to-CFO jump. The non-technical ones decide the offer more often than the technical ones do; the field notes are the things nobody asks you and everybody assumes you already know.

    ◆ Technical
    Walk me through how a $1M inventory write-down flows through all three statements.Amazon · Walmart · Majid Al Futtaim
    P&L: COGS up $1M, so gross profit, EBIT and pre-tax income each fall $1M. At a 25% rate, net income falls $750K. Cash flow: start from net income −$750K, add back the $1M non-cash write-down, so operating cash flow rises $250K — the tax shield. Balance sheet: inventory −$1M, cash +$250K, so assets fall $750K; retained earnings fall $750K. It balances. The point they are testing: you know the write-down is non-cash and that the only real cash effect is tax.
    How do you value a new store before signing a 10-year lease?IKEA · Majid Al Futtaim · Costco
    Build an unlevered free cash flow model over the lease term. Revenue from catchment population, comparable sales density and a ramp curve — never assume year-one maturity. Deduct COGS, rent with escalation clauses, staff, and allocated overhead only if genuinely incremental. Capex is fit-out plus initial inventory. Discount at WACC and test IRR versus hurdle, payback, and NPV. Then sensitise the two variables that actually decide it: year-one sales and the ramp. A store that only clears the hurdle at 100% of forecast is a no.
    ◆ Everything else
    How do you stay current technically while managing?Big Four partners · Group CFOs
    Be specific and verifiable. Name the standard you last read in full and why, the technical update you attended, the thing you built yourself recently rather than delegated. Vague answers about 'reading widely' land badly with technical interviewers. The strongest version connects it to a decision: 'I went back through IFRS 16 properly when we hit the lease modification question on the Indonesian stores, and that changed how we structured the renewal.'
    Where do you think retail is heading in the next five years?Gulf retail boards · Search firms
    Have a view and defend it with numbers, not trends. Something like: physical stores stop being judged on their own sales as fulfilment and returns blur the channels, which breaks conventional store P&L and forces a catchment-level view; margin pressure shifts from price to cost-to-serve; and in the Gulf specifically, corporate tax and e-invoicing professionalise finance functions that were previously light. Then say what you would do about it in the role you are interviewing for.
    ◆ From the field
    Know your break-even by store, not by chain.The analysis that gets you noticed
    A chain at group-level profitability can be carrying a quarter of its estate below contribution. Compute store contribution — gross profit less rent, staff and directly attributable fixed cost — and rank it. Then, before closing anything: is the lease committed, what does the store contribute to online fulfilment in its catchment, and what is the exit cost. A negative-contribution store with four years left on a non-cancellable lease is usually cheaper to run than to close.
    VAT in the GCC is a cash-flow instrument, not just a compliance task.UAE · KSA · Oman
    Registration thresholds, filing frequency and the timing of input recovery all move working capital. A group filing quarterly rather than monthly holds recoverable input VAT longer than it needs to. Know the filing calendar in every jurisdiction the group trades in, and know which entity holds the recoverable balance — a refund stuck in the wrong entity is cash you own and cannot use.

    05 / BOOK

    Atomic Habits.

    James Clear · chapter 1 of 48. One chapter a day, and where the book has been read properly, the whole thing underneath it — the argument in a dozen points, what changes in your head after reading it, and how it lands on an FP&A desk rather than in general.

    Atomic Habits · James Clear · 1/48

    The 1% Rule: Why Small Gains Compound Into Extraordinary Results

    If you get 1% better every day for a year, you end up 37 times better. If you get 1% worse, you decay to nearly zero. Most people overestimate what they can do in a day and underestimate what they can do in a year of consistent 1% improvements. Clear's central insight: you don't rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems. Goals are for direction. Systems are for progress.

    Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement.
    Today's actionIdentify one tiny habit you can improve by 1% today. Stack it onto an existing behaviour: after [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT].
    The whole book · 12 points
    1. You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems.
    2. 1% better daily compounds to 37x in a year. 1% worse decays to 0.03x.
    3. Habits form on a four-step loop: cue → craving → response → reward.
    4. Build good habits: make it obvious, attractive, easy, satisfying. Invert all four to break one.
    5. Identity drives behaviour, not the reverse. 'I don't smoke' beats 'I'm trying to quit'.
    6. Every action is a vote for the person you are becoming. Majority wins, not unanimity.
    7. The two-minute rule: a new habit must take under two minutes to start.
    8. Environment beats willpower. Motivation is overrated; friction is decisive.
    9. Habit stacking: after [existing habit], I will [new habit]. The old habit becomes the cue.
    10. You need a plateau of latent potential. Results lag effort — most people quit inside the lag.
    11. Never miss twice. One miss is an accident. Two is the start of a new habit.
    12. Measure what you want to repeat. An unrecorded habit drifts within three weeks.
    What actually changes in your head
    • Discipline is a design problem, not a character problem.
    • The gap between effort and visible result is where nearly everyone quits.
    • Systems survive bad days. Motivation does not.
    • Tracking is not admin. Tracking is the habit.
    Examples
    • British Cycling went from one Tour de France win in 110 years to five in six years by stacking 1% gains — pillow hygiene, hand-washing, bike paint for dust visibility.
    • Clear's own recovery: a bat to the face at 15, two years of rebuilding, then an academic All-American — built entirely on sleep and study routines, not talent.
    • Twice-weekly gym attendance beats a 90-minute session once a month, even though the monthly total is similar. Frequency builds identity; volume does not.
    How to adapt it into your life
    • Score one caption per day before posting. The score is the habit, not the post.
    • Two-minute rule on the Dubai job hunt: open the tracker and add one company. That is the whole commitment.
    • SIP on auto-debit the day salary lands. Remove the monthly decision entirely.
    • Never miss twice on the daily brief. One skipped morning is fine. Two means the system broke.

    Opening Principle #3: Castle Early

    King safety is non-negotiable in the opening. Castle within the first 10 moves. An uncastled king in the centre is a target. After castling, your king is protected by 3 pawns and 2 corner squares. Rooks also connect after castling. Rule: castle before you attack.
